# Kitsunebi Research Series — Open Working Papers

**22 October 2026 | Preprints / research proposals, NOT peer-reviewed research.** No participant studies or quantitative results have been performed for these manuscripts. Their hypotheses are proposed; no claims of effectiveness or clinical benefit are made.

Kitsunebi builds on internal design questions associated with *The Chaotic Archive* and *The Sanctuary in the Machine*. The original full texts were not present in the inspected `website` repository and have **not** been silently recreated, overwritten or represented as read. The manuscripts below are new, standalone working papers that should be reconciled with the original research archive once located.

## Papers

1. [Semantic Invariance and User-Authored Interface Dialects](paper-01-semantic-invariance.md) — formal grammar, invariants, reference implementation and falsifiable evaluation.
2. [Sanctuary Without Paternalism](paper-02-sanctuary.md) — sensory comfort, accessibility, user agency and explicit adaptation.
3. [Coherent Pluralism in Cross-Platform Design Languages](paper-03-coherent-pluralism.md) — interoperability, governance and collective self-authorship.

## Publication integrity rules

- Treat **design rationale**, **technical implementation**, **reviewed literature**, and **empirical findings** as separate evidence categories.
- Never imply sampling, interviews, statistically significant findings or formal ethics approval that did not occur.
- Record study preregistration before confirmatory testing, and publish excluded data criteria.
- Credit all contributors and research participants; obtain consent; respect culturally situated symbols and Māori data sovereignty in Aotearoa contexts.
- Use permanent versions, dates, archived sources and transparent licences before external publication.
- Cite relevant standards accurately: W3C WCAG 2.2; W3C DTCG 2025.10; ISO 9241-210:2019 (verify access and full text separately); Deci & Ryan (2000); Norman (2013).
- Design experiments for people whose access needs differ from the designers'. A self-selected group of expert Linux users alone is not representative.

See [ethics and methods](methods-and-ethics.md) and [language specification](../docs/language-specification.md).
